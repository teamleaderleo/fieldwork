## In simple words

The managed journal forwarder can be made to keep its retained-record population bounded while one POST is stuck. The owned candidate lets the current in-flight batch continue, permits one bounded pending batch behind it, then stops reading additional journal records from session sockets until the flusher releases capacity. It preserves records by applying backpressure instead of dropping them.

The result changes the stalled-memory curve dramatically. In the original current-main harness, a single stuck POST allowed every subscribed producer to append into one shared pending pool, pushing resident memory to roughly 149 MiB at 128 active sessions. With one pending pool capped at 100 records or 4 MiB of encoded record payload, the same fixed-duration stalled experiment stayed around 9–16 MiB from 10 through 128 active sessions.

## Candidate mechanics

The executable candidate is retained on the owned fork as:

`scripts/fieldwork/cmux-resource-collapse/journal_backpressure.patch`

It is applied against the exact upstream-derived journal source during CI. The candidate:

- keeps `MAX_BATCH_RECORDS=100` and `MAX_BATCH_BODY_BYTES=4 MiB`;
- adds a bounded pending-pool record budget of 100 records;
- adds a bounded pending-pool encoded-record byte budget of 4 MiB;
- tracks pending record count and bytes explicitly;
- adds a `Notify` used by session readers waiting for pool capacity;
- changes the session ingestion path to wait when one POST is active and the next pending pool has reached its budget;
- wakes blocked session readers when the current POST completes and pending capacity becomes available;
- retains the existing body-splitting and cursor-ack logic.

The patch adds two focused tests:

1. `busy_pool_backpressures_after_one_bounded_pending_batch`
2. `busy_pool_byte_budget_bounds_large_records`

## Cleanup

Every stalled candidate case returned process FD count to 12 after cancellation. RSS remained near each run's high-water, consistent with allocator page retention; this receipt does not claim a live-object leak after teardown.

The fake producers sometimes reported `Broken pipe` during cancellation because the forwarder closes session sockets while synthetic writers are still attempting their high-volume stream. Those messages occur during the explicit cleanup phase and did not fail the harness.

## Tradeoff and remaining tuning question

The conservative 100-record pending budget creates many more POSTs during a burst: 150 requests for the 15,000-record healthy control versus 9 on current main.

A larger but bounded pending budget could reduce POST cardinality while retaining the key memory property. Any tuning should preserve a byte ceiling, explicit admission backpressure, order/cursor behavior, and the same stalled scaling experiment. The current 100-record candidate is therefore a strong correctness/performance discriminator and a conservative implementation candidate, while request-count tuning remains open.

There is also a small transient race around flusher completion where a waking producer may append just as `flushing` changes state; the total remains bounded, but a polished production version should make the capacity guard exact across both busy and handoff states.

## Consequence

Current main: degraded journal forwarding can become memory growth proportional to aggregate production during endpoint failure, potentially reaching resource exhaustion or relay outage.

Candidate: resource use remains bounded in the forwarder; sustained outage manifests as backpressure to session journal sockets instead of unbounded retained heap.
