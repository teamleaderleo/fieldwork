## In simple words

Two resource-collapse mechanisms were reproduced and reduced to their owning operations.

First, the managed journal forwarder lets many session producers accumulate behind one stalled POST. The bend comes from aggregate producer rate feeding one shared pending pool while the single flusher owns a retrying batch. Current main has no aggregate retained-record ceiling during that busy interval. Live RSS climbed from about 8.6 MiB at one stalled producer to about 149 MiB at 128 active producers in the owned harness. A bounded-pending-pool candidate flattened the same family of stalled runs to about 9–16 MiB and pushed additional pressure back into session sockets.

Second, the macOS remote-daemon stdio client can let one physical `FileHandle.write` hold the process-wide daemon write lane before normal RPC response deadlines begin. The failure reproduces with one queued caller. Fire-and-forget PTY notifications have the same owner and originally had no response-side watchdog at all. A one-second non-WebSocket write-liveness owner now covers both request/response writes and notifications on the owned fork; 1 / 10 / 50 / 200 scaling probes pass for both families.

The latest upstream head still contains the exact audited implementation blobs for both mechanisms, so these conclusions remain current as of `594eb0461e0ae4d57a99180e19097cea5e5091e0`.

## Finding 1 — journal single-consumer retention

### Exact owner

`cmux-tui/crates/chatmux-relay/src/journal_forwarder.rs`

Latest-upstream blob checked: `9b4551770e807084c48c2cdbe94f15f2cf2358e0`.

### Scaling variable

Number of concurrently producing subscribed sessions, up to the source discovery cap of 128.

### Baseline / current-main failure curve

Stalled endpoint, 300 records/session, 1024-byte payload, one HTTP request held open:

| requested | active | loaded RSS KiB | loaded FDs | cleanup FDs |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 1 | 8,572 | 18 | 12 |
| 10 | 10 | 18,332 | 45 | 12 |
| 50 | 50 | 62,072 | 165 | 12 |
| 128 | 128 | 148,568 | 399 | 12 |
| 200 | 128 | 146,456 | 471 | 12 |

Exactly one POST was observed in each stalled run.

### Failure threshold

The source-level threshold is the first record beyond the nominal pending-batch threshold while a POST is already active: one batch is in flight, 100 more records fill the shared pending pool, and the 101st additional record demonstrates that the 100-record threshold is no longer an aggregate retention bound.

The operational knee is where aggregate arrival rate approaches or exceeds the one flusher's service rate. During a stalled POST, service rate is zero and retained records grow with aggregate production until external limits intervene.

### Saturating resource

Retained journal record state / resident heap behind one in-flight POST. FDs scale with active session sockets but return to the cleanup baseline in the harness.

### Smallest amplification sequence

1. 100 records trigger a shared flush.
2. The POST remains in flight or retry.
3. Another 100 records arrive and stay in `pool.pending`.
4. More records continue to append because `flushing` turns the threshold into `flush_again=true` rather than a capacity limit.

### Surviving state after load ceases

Session/socket FDs converged to 12 after cancellation in every measured case. RSS stayed near high-water; allocator retention prevents using that alone as live-object leak evidence.

### Clean-shutdown comparison

The forwarder cancels, aborts and joins session workers, and closes the session sockets. FD convergence succeeded at every tested scale.

### Candidate

Executable patch artifact:

`scripts/fieldwork/cmux-resource-collapse/journal_backpressure.patch`

The candidate permits one bounded pending pool behind the active POST, then makes session readers wait for capacity. Current limits are 100 pending records or 4 MiB encoded-record bytes.

Unit result: 27 journal-forwarder tests passed, including two new backpressure tests.

Candidate stalled fixed-duration curve:

| requested | active | generated by measurement | loaded RSS KiB | cleanup FDs |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 1 | 294 | 8,680 | 12 |
| 10 | 10 | 1,030 | 9,184 | 12 |
| 50 | 50 | 4,813 | 11,716 | 12 |
| 128 | 128 | 12,025 | 16,280 | 12 |
| 200 | 128 | 12,080 | 15,076 | 12 |

Every stalled candidate case still had exactly one HTTP request. Producers remained incomplete at measurement, demonstrating that excess pressure had moved to the producer/socket boundary instead of retained forwarder heap.

Healthy 50-session burst tradeoff:

- current-main control: ~103 MiB loaded RSS, 9 POSTs for 15,000 records;
- candidate: ~15 MiB loaded RSS, 150 POSTs for 15,000 records.

The conservative 100-record cap therefore solves the retention curve at the cost of request cardinality. A polished production version can tune the bounded pending count while keeping a hard byte ceiling and the same backpressure invariant.

### Consequence

Degradation progressing to memory exhaustion / relay outage under sustained endpoint failure or sufficiently high aggregate journal production. Delivery lag also grows without convergence when arrival rate exceeds recovered service rate.

## Finding 2 — remote-daemon stdio write-lane liveness

### Exact owner

`Packages/macOS/CmuxRemoteDaemon/Sources/CmuxRemoteDaemon/Client/RemoteDaemonRPCClient+RPC.swift`

Latest-upstream blob checked: `0eddc4847913125a2804e00487d43c47c9454b98`.

### Scaling variable

Number of synchronous daemon operations or PTY notification writers queued behind one physical stdio write whose peer remains alive but ceases reading.

### Baseline / failure threshold

Responsive control: 200 concurrent `hello` callers completed in 0.727 seconds on the original red run.

Physical-stall red: failure already at N=1. A caller configured with a 50 ms RPC response timeout remained blocked past the 750 ms probe bound because it had not yet reached the response-wait code.

### Saturating resource

One global serialized daemon write lane, plus waiting caller work and pending-call entries for request/response RPCs. PTY bridge callers also retain their per-bridge RPC workers while notification writes are stuck.

### Smallest amplification sequence

Request/response:

1. remote helper answers startup;
2. helper stays alive and stops reading stdin;
3. one write fills the pipe and blocks inside `FileHandle.write`;
4. the next RPC registers a pending call and waits for the same write lane;
5. its ordinary response timeout has yet to start.

Notification-only:

1. helper answers startup and stops reading;
2. one allowed 256 KiB `pty.write` notification blocks the shared stdio writer;
3. later PTY writers queue behind it;
4. no response phase exists to supply a later watchdog.

### Clean-shutdown comparison

Direct `client.stop()` broke the physical writer and released queued caller populations in the red harness.

### Owned production candidate

Non-WebSocket request/response and notification writes now share a one-second write-liveness budget on the owned branch. Existing response timeouts remain separate and begin after a healthy request write completes. WebSocket behavior keeps its previous path.

Green evidence:

- broad `CmuxRemoteDaemon` package tests outside a known upstream parallel-test timing failure pass;
- each timeout-isolation test passes when run alone;
- responsive 200-caller control passes;
- physical request-write stall passes at 1 / 10 / 50 / 200 callers;
- notification-only 256 KiB PTY-write stall passes at 1 / 10 / 50 / 200 callers.

The untouched upstream control reproduced the same parallel timeout-test failures seen during broad candidate runs, so those failures are classified as pre-existing test interference rather than candidate regression evidence.

### Consequence

Remote tunnel outage, stranded operations and terminal-input workers, duplicated waiting work during bridge replacement/reconnect, and growing thread/work-item pressure as logical operations multiply behind the same blocked writer.

### Residual

`callIfIdle` still enters the global write queue directly. Its practical exposure is smaller because the keepalive frame is tiny and admission occurs only when no application response is pending. It remains a source-visible liveness edge without a dedicated runtime red in this scout.

## Other areas

The following paths showed explicit ownership/bounds and serve as controls rather than primary collapse findings:

- journal hook workers: bounded worker/admission counts;
- machine-provider subprocesses: process-group termination, escalation, wait, stderr-worker join;
- `SharedLiveAgentIndex`: bounded loader replacement, watch-install work and FD budget;
- terminal hosts: explicit live-output/state/parser byte and frame ceilings;
- SSH reconnect timer: replacement-owned; reachability probe can overlap stale detached work but each probe has bounded subprocess/TCP deadlines.

A remaining runtime candidate is proxy remote-to-local `NWConnection.send` completion retention under a slow local consumer.

## Evidence limits

- Runtime probes use synthetic local peers on GitHub-hosted Ubuntu/macOS while exercising real cmux implementation code.
- Journal cleanup RSS is allocator-sensitive; FD convergence and explicit pool bounds are stronger evidence.
- The journal candidate is retained as an executable patch artifact rather than written into the fork's production Rust source file.
- The RPC candidate is written into the owned fork production source and has owned-fork macOS execution.
- Neither candidate has upstream review, production deployment, or operational latency distribution data.
- Upstream remained read-only and no maintainers were contacted.

## Recommendation

Retain both as high-priority owned-fork candidates. The RPC write-liveness fix is the cleaner contribution-sized change and already has red/green request and notification scaling evidence. The journal backpressure fix has the stronger resource-collapse consequence and a decisive curve improvement, with one remaining tuning choice: acceptable bounded pending depth versus POST cardinality.
